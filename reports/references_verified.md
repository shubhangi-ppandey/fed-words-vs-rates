## C. Novelty assessment and positioning (adopted 2026-10-16)

- **Crowded:** the general claim that pretrained or fine-tuned language models leak future information.
  A chronological split on the TDW corpus has also been done, but only for classification accuracy (Shah et al. 2023 App. D; Schlee et al. 2026).
- **Apparently new:** quantifying how label and fine-tuning look-ahead **propagates into an econometric estimate**, the
  30-minute market response to FOMC tone surprises net of high-frequency policy surprises. The design adds
  a 2×2 {TF-IDF, RoBERTa-large} × {full-sample, chronological} and an untouched 2023–2026 holdout.
- **Positioning:** "quantifying how look-ahead bias distorts monetary-communication event-study estimates", not "discovering look-ahead bias".
  The title was changed from "…Without Look-Ahead Bias" to "**Quantifying Look-Ahead Bias in Measured Federal Reserve Communication Effects**".
- **Referee points to state up front as limitations:**
  1. RoBERTa's general pretraining corpus (≤2019) still leaks for older statements. The TF-IDF arm has no pretraining and partly separates
     the two channels. A ChronoBERT arm (He et al. 2026) would separate them fully; this is a possible extension E3.
  2. The TDW labels were assigned in 2022–23 with hindsight; chronological ordering of the labels does not remove the annotators' hindsight.
  3. The holdout has no corpus labels. 2023–2026 statements are **scored** by models trained on all 1996–2022 labels.
     For holdout statements, M2 and M3 (cutoff 2023) therefore use the same label set: both are label-look-ahead-free there by construction.
     The look-ahead comparison is an in-sample (1999–2022) object; the holdout tests whether the estimated effect generalises.
  4. Acosta et al. (2026): press conferences now dominate the market response. Our 30-min window captures the statement only, so the
     daily-window robustness check (which includes the presser) matters for the 2019+ period.
